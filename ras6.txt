An old man walked the beach each dawn, not with a metal detector, but with a gentle, searching gaze. His pockets bulged not with treasure, but with forgotten items: a single child’s flip-flop, a sun-bleached toy soldier, a spectacles case filled with sand. He would clean them and place them on a certain log near the dunes. By afternoon, they were always gone. He wasn’t sure who collected them, or if they ever found their original owners. He simply believed that every lost thing deserved a moment of care, a pause between being missed and being found, or being let go.
